The second annual SEMA ADAS Symposium confirmed what many shops already know: OEM calibration guidance is incomplete and inconsistent. Sixty-three attendees from 47 organizations spent the day on it. Here's what matters for your bay.

TRAINING

1. SEMA ADAS Symposium confirms OEM calibration guidance gaps

Sixty-three attendees from 47 organizations, including automakers and aftermarket leaders, convened to discuss calibration, testing, and serviceability. The session underscored a persistent industry pain point: incomplete and inconsistent OEM documentation for ADAS repair. Shops reported that vague or missing calibration specs force guesswork on vehicle performance and customer safety.

Mark says: Document every OEM ambiguity you hit this week. This data builds the case for better specs in your next insurer negotiation.

Source: BodyShop Business →

TRAINING

2. I-CAR RTS develops guidance after DEG inquiry signals repair gap

A Data Enhancement Gateway inquiry triggered I-CAR's Repairability Technical Support team to build new technical guidance on a specific repair procedure. The move signals that the industry is actively mapping holes in current OEM and repair documentation. This guidance will likely become a reference tool shops cite in estimates and denials.

Mark says: Watch I-CAR's bulletin board this week. New RTS guidance is a 30-second cite for any related supplement fight.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

OEM

3. Hyundai Motor Group mandates Level 2+ by 2028, standardizes sensors

Hyundai announced it will put NVIDIA-based Level 2+ automated driving systems into production in the first half of 2028, with Level 2++ systems in the second half. The group is also standardizing sensor platforms across its brands, Kia and Genesis. This standardization simplifies your calibration playbook but increases the volume of ADAS work on newer models hitting collision bays in 24 months.

Mark says: Start building your Hyundai/Kia/Genesis Level 2+ playbook now. You'll need it in 18 months.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INDUSTRY

4. eBay bans airbag and inflator sales effective Sept. 24

eBay announced it will prohibit the sale of airbags and inflators on its platform starting September 24. The move targets the counterfeit parts pipeline that has contributed to safety failures and customer liability exposure. This reduces a major source of gray-market airbags entering the repair supply chain.

Mark says: Alert your parts suppliers and estimators: counterfeit airbag availability drops Sept. 24. Source OEM airbags only.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

RECALL

5. Carfax: 27% of rideshare and taxi fleets carry unfixed recalls

Carfax examined taxis and rideshare vehicles across 16 major U.S. cities and Maryland, finding at least one unfixed safety recall on about 27 percent of them. Many of these recalls involve ADAS and safety-critical systems. The finding highlights the work collision shops can capture by running recall checks on every incoming vehicle and flagging drivers to OEM dealers before they return to the road.

Mark says: Add recall lookup to your check-in walkthrough. High-mileage commercial vehicles are a goldmine for this play.

Source: CollisionWeek →
