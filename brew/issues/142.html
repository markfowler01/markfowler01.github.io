Hyundai just tightened its non-OEM parts language in a way that matters to your estimates and your relationships with insurers. Meanwhile, AAA's latest intersection AEB testing shows what ADAS can't do yet, and why documentation matters.

OEM

1. Hyundai strengthens non-OEM parts ban in collision repair

Hyundai has revised its official position statement on OEM1Stop to explicitly reject non-OEM and aftermarket parts in collision repair. The language is tougher than before. This directly affects your estimates, supplement negotiations, and what you can recommend to insurers without pushback. File this reference now before the next Hyundai job walks in.

Mark says: Cite Hyundai's OEM1Stop statement on every Hyundai supplement to block insurer part-cost pushback.

ADAS

2. AAA finds intersection AEB systems have real limits

AAA tested four 2026 model-year vehicles equipped with intersection automatic emergency braking and found performance gaps in real-world scenarios. The systems did not catch all collision angles or speeds. This matters because customers and adjusters assume AEB prevents every crash. Document what ADAS does and doesn't prevent in your calibration reports.

Mark says: Reference AAA test results in customer walkthroughs and adjuster calls when AEB limitations affect injury or liability assumptions.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

OEM

3. Shop satisfaction with OEM certification drops for second year

Shops' satisfaction with OEM certification programs declined for the second consecutive year, according to Autobody News. Rising costs, training demands, and vague parts approval guidelines are frustration points. OEM programs remain necessary, but the relationship is under strain. This affects your team retention and operating margins.

Mark says: Track your certification costs against earnings and flag unreasonable OEM demands at SCRS or state association level.

Source: Autobody News →

INSURANCE

4. Liberty Mutual, two insurers refund $122.7M in excess Florida auto profits

Florida regulators ordered three auto insurers, including Liberty Mutual, to return a combined $122.7 million in excess profits to policyholders. This brings Florida's 2026 excess-profit refunds to over $168 million. Regulatory pressure on insurer profitability is rising nationally. Watch for this dynamic to affect claim reductions and supplement denials in your state.

Mark says: Expect tighter claim management and supplement scrutiny as insurers face state-level profit caps and refund mandates.

Source: CollisionWeek →

OEM

5. Mercedes gets German approval for point-to-point autonomous driving

Mercedes-Benz received KBA approval in Germany for MB.DRIVE ASSIST PRO, making it the first manufacturer approved for point-to-point assisted driving under UN Regulation No. 157. This marks a step toward higher autonomy levels in production vehicles. As ADAS and autonomous systems advance, recalibration procedures and equipment specs will evolve. Stay tuned to OEM bulletins for procedure changes.

Mark says: Monitor Mercedes TSBs and equipment lists closely as higher-autonomy systems enter the North American market.
