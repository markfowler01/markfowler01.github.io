State Farm just agreed to a $45 million Oklahoma settlement for claims handling. Meanwhile, AAA testing found that three of four vehicles fail to prevent crashes at intersections. Both matter to your bottom line.

INSURANCE

1. State Farm settles Oklahoma claims handling for $45M

The Oklahoma Insurance Department investigated State Farm Fire and Casualty Company for handling practices and secured a settlement worth $45 million. This follows similar actions in other states and signals regulators are watching insurer conduct more closely. For shops, this trend supports stronger negotiating positions on supplements and repair scope.

Mark says: Reference this in supplement disputes; cite regulatory pressure when insurers deny scope creep.

ADAS

2. AAA finds three of four vehicles fail intersection AEB test

AAA tested automatic emergency braking in intersection scenarios (turning into oncoming traffic, left turns without signal). Only the Tesla Model Y avoided collision in every run. The Hyundai Tucson did not respond in left-turn scenarios. This gap matters because intersection crashes are common and ADAS systems are not yet reliable in these conditions.

Mark says: Tell customers their ADAS calibration doesn't prevent every crash; manage expectations on what systems actually do.

Source: CollisionWeek →

OEM

3. Mitchell 1 ProDemand+ connects collision and mechanical repair data

Mitchell 1 launched ProDemand+, an add-on that embeds OEM collision repair information into its ProDemand mechanical platform. This reduces the need to toggle between systems when diagnosing related repair work. Shops using Mitchell can now cross-reference collision procedures with mechanical service bulletins in one place.

Mark says: If you run Mitchell, test ProDemand+ this week to see if it speeds up your estimate and repair planning.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INDUSTRY

4. Boyd Q3 sales up 28% to $1.01B; total loss momentum builds

Boyd Group reported Q3 sales of $1.01 billion, up 28.2% year-over-year, with same-store sales growth of 2.2%. CEO Brian Kaner noted positive momentum in pushing total losses down. This signals the market is shifting away from total calls and toward more repairable damage, which benefits independent shops competing for work.

Mark says: Repairable claims are rising; invest in ADAS and mechanical capabilities to capture more complex work from insurers.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INSURANCE

5. Texas finalizes right-to-appraisal rules with new protections

The Texas Department of Insurance adopted final appraisal rules after reviewing over 1,000 public comments. Changes were made to strengthen the process and clarify shop rights. Texas shops now have firmer legal ground to invoke appraisal when insurers deny repair scope or supplement requests.

Mark says: If you operate in Texas, review the final rule and use it to back appraisal threats in supplement disputes.
