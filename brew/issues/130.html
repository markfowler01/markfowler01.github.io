RECALL

1. Toyota Sienna, Ford F-Series, Dodge Durango recalls active

Toyota Sienna seat back, F-Series tailgate cable, and Durango taillight recalls are in effect. Check your intake workflow this week to flag these VINs early. Get your service writers pulling these during check-in so you're not caught mid-repair.

Mark says: Flag these on every Toyota, Ford, Dodge intake this week; prevents estimate rework and customer callbacks later.

2. Opus, asTech, BlueDriver MAX release ADAS and J2534 tools at SEMA

Three diagnostics players launched new collision tools: ADAS MAP add-ons and a J2534 scan interface. The combined offering tightens your diagnostic workflow and speeds calibration handoff. If you're running mixed OEM platforms, this cuts manual cable swaps.

Mark says: If you're still hunting for a unified scan strategy, pull specs on these three before you commit to a platform.

OEM

3. Celette AI Webtools wins SEMA best collision product award

Celette's AI platform took top honors for new collision repair and refinish product at SEMA 2026. The tool integrates into your estimating and workflow. Worth a demo if you're still using legacy repair procedure lookups.

Mark says: SEMA jury picked this for repair efficiency; worth a call if your procedure pulls are slowing your estimators down.

INDUSTRY

4. Mitchell releases AI photo estimating to independent shops; Crash Champions goes network-wide

Mitchell's intelligent estimating tool, already used by insurers, is now open to multi-shop and single-shop repairers. It drafts preliminary estimates from photos for your estimator to refine. Crash Champions is rolling it across their network immediately.

Mark says: If you're losing photo estimate speed to your MSO competitors, this closes the gap; ask Mitchell for a trial.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INSURANCE

5. Progressive reports collision severity up 4%, frequency down 3% in Q3

Progressive's latest filing shows claims are getting pricier per accident but happen less often. The insurer is testing AI agents for shopping and pricing. Watch for downward supplement pressure as insurers tighten margins.

Mark says: Severity is rising but frequency is falling; expect tighter insurer scrutiny on supplements and labor rates going forward.
