Toyota is recalling 228,000 US vehicles for unsecured seat backs. Ford released updated SRS guidance this week. And California storage and airbag regulations are set to take effect January 1 unless blocked. Three items that will land on your intake sheets this month.

RECALL

1. Toyota recalls 228K US vehicles over unsecured seat back risk

NHTSA confirmed a recall affecting more than 228,000 Toyota vehicles in the US over an unsecured seat back hazard. This is a high-volume recall that will funnel into shops immediately. Pull the recall number and the affected model years into your check-in walkthrough this week. Customers will call ahead once the notice lands in their mailbox.

Mark says: Add this recall lookup to your check-in sheet; 228K units means steady phone volume starting this week and next.

Source: Reuters / NHTSA →

TSB

2. Ford updates SRS guidance for on-target repairs

Ford released updated SRS (Supplemental Restraint System) guidance this week aimed at steering shops toward correct on-target repair procedures. If you work on Fords with SRS damage, file this bulletin now. It clarifies what on-target means in Ford's language and keeps you compliant when supplements land.

Mark says: Print the Ford SRS bulletin and tape it to your estimating workstation; reference it on every SRS supplement.

Source: Autobody News →

INDUSTRY

3. California storage and airbag regs take effect Jan. 1

The California Bureau of Automotive Repair (BAR) storage fee and airbag safety regulations are set to go live January 1, pending final Office of Administrative Law approval. These are regulatory changes that will affect your intake process, storage pricing, and airbag handling procedures. If you work in California, read the full regs now and build your compliance checklist before year-end.

Mark says: California shops: build storage fee and airbag handling procedures now; Jan. 1 comes fast.

Source: Repairer Driven News / SCRS →

TSB

4. AkzoNobel expands basecoat blending area guidance in updated bulletin

AkzoNobel released an updated technical service bulletin that significantly expands the recommended area for effective basecoat color blends. The new guidance changes what shops have been doing for blending work. This affects your paint department directly and may require re-training your spray techs. Get the bulletin to your refinish lead this week.

Mark says: Pull the AkzoNobel bulletin and walk your paint team through the new blending area; update your standard work.

Source: Repairer Driven News / SCRS →

OEM

5. ALLDATA adds OEM documentation and audit trail to Repair Planner

ALLDATA rolled out new features that let you attach OEM procedures directly to estimate line items and maintain a time-stamped audit trail of your repair research. This is a soft upgrade but it matters for defense: when an insurer challenges your labor or procedure, you can prove which OEM doc you cited and when. If you use ALLDATA, turn this feature on and start using it on every supplement.

Mark says: Enable the OEM documentation and audit trail features in ALLDATA; attach procedures to every supplement line.
