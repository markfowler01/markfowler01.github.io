1. GM, Ford engineers on ADAS repair procedures at SEMA

Active safety engineers from General Motors and Ford will present at the SCRS OEM Summit during the SEMA Show next month. They'll walk through the engineering decisions that influence vehicle safety before, during, and after collision repair. This is a direct pipeline to why OEMs write their procedures the way they do.

2. Static vs. dynamic calibration: know the difference

BodyShop Business published a primer on static and dynamic ADAS calibrations today. The piece cuts through the confusion on when to use each method and why it matters for the vehicle in front of you. Understanding which method applies to each make and model is table stakes for shops handling ADAS repairs.

Mark says: Pull this article and put it in your tech folder. Reference it before every ADAS write-up to confirm your approach.

RECALL

3. Hyundai recalls 94,600 US vehicles over fire risk

Hyundai is recalling over 94,600 US vehicles due to fire risk from overheating, according to NHTSA. The scope is large and the urgency is high. Check recall databases before handing keys back on any Hyundai in your bay this week.

Mark says: Run Hyundai check-ins against NHTSA recall database this week. Flag any affected vehicles before delivery.

Source: Reuters / NHTSA →

INSURANCE

4. Vehicle repair costs up 5.8% year-over-year

Vehicle maintenance and repair prices jumped 5.8% compared to last year, pushing the auto insurance index higher. Parts costs are climbing due to tariffs and OEM supply responses. Expect more supplement requests and pushback from carriers on your estimates.

Mark says: Document parts cost increases in every estimate. When carriers deny supplements, cite the 5.8% rise and attach proof of your invoices.

Source: Autobody News →
