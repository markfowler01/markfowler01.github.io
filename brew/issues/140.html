Toyota's pulling 314,000 vehicles for Takata airbags, and Oklahoma just documented State Farm violating its claims settlement laws. Both hit your desk this week.

RECALL

1. Toyota recalls 314K vehicles for Takata airbag defect

Toyota and Lexus are voluntarily recalling 314,000 U.S. vehicles with Takata front passenger airbags. This is a safety-critical repair. Takata is bankrupt, so parts availability and customer communication matter. Add this to your intake VIN lookup immediately so customers know before they drive in.

Mark says: Pull NHTSA recall lookup on every Toyota and Lexus intake this week. Flag any matching VINs before the customer leaves the lot.

INSURANCE

2. Oklahoma finds State Farm violated unfair settlement laws

The Oklahoma Insurance Department issued a market examination report documenting multiple violations of the state's Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act by State Farm. This is regulatory ammunition. When State Farm denies your supplement or undercuts a scope, cite this violation record in your rebuttal.

Mark says: Screenshot the Oklahoma report. Attach it to every State Farm supplement denial in your state as proof of their settlement practices pattern.

OEM

3. Mercedes-Benz brings hands-free and point-to-point driving to U.S. in 2027

Mercedes is expanding its MB.DRIVE portfolio to the U.S., adding advanced point-to-point driver assistance and hands-free highway driving. This means more Level 3 autonomous systems hitting your bay starting next year. Recalibration complexity and sensor requirements will jump.

Mark says: Start pulling Mercedes technical bulletins now. Level 3 driving systems require new calibration procedures and sensor positioning accuracy.

TRAINING

4. State Farm formally recommends I-CAR Gold Class certification

State Farm is formally recommending I-CAR Gold Class certification for collision repair shops in its Select Service program, supplementing existing welding requirements. This is not a mandate yet, but the pressure is real. Shops without Gold Class will face estimate friction on State Farm jobs.

Mark says: If you're not Gold Class yet, treat this as a 90-day window before it becomes a de facto requirement for State Farm work.

Source: CollisionWeek →

OEM

5. Hyundai reinforces non-OEM parts prohibition for collision repairs

Hyundai issued a revised position statement refusing approval for counterfeit, gray market, aftermarket, recycled, or non-OEM parts on collision repairs. This is a hard line from Hyundai. Estimate supplements using non-OEM parts will be denied. Prioritize OEM sourcing on Hyundai jobs or face customer friction.

Mark says: Educate your estimators: Hyundai jobs require OEM parts front-to-back. No aftermarket alternatives. Build that into your supplement write-up.

🛠 Operator's Corner
For the folks who run a calibration business

Say No in Writing Before You Pull That Target

Some cars show up with a pulled fender, a fresh alignment that never got checked, or a code still lit on the dash. Running a calibration on that car does not fix anything, and your name is on the report when something goes wrong later. A short written refusal, handed to the driver and kept in your records, shows exactly what you found and why you stopped. It protects the driver too, because they leave knowing the car is not ready instead of thinking everything is fine.

▶ This week: Build a one-page pre-calibration checklist that covers alignment status, ride height, glass condition, and active codes, and add a signature line so the customer acknowledges any failed item before you pack up and leave.
