Two states are moving fast to rein in insurers. New Jersey just introduced a bill barring AI from making final claim denials, and Ohio shops are rallying against legislation that could gut assignment of benefits. Both matter to your bottom line.

INSURANCE

1. New Jersey targets AI-powered claim denials with new legislation

A New Jersey lawmaker introduced a bill that would prohibit insurers from using AI to make final claim denials on auto, homeowners, and flood coverage. The legislation focuses specifically on the final denial decision, not the claim investigation itself. This is the first state-level move to restrict AI in the denial process. If it passes, it sets a template other states will likely follow.

Mark says: File this for your next denied ADAS calibration claim. NJ precedent will embolden appeals in your state.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INDUSTRY

2. Ohio shops unite against SB 306 assignment and storage fee provisions

The Ohio Autobody Association is asking the collision industry to oppose Senate Bill 306, citing concerns over assignment of benefits language, storage fee language, insurer access to repair facilities, and repair contract terms. OABA says the bill as drafted could weaken independent shops' ability to collect payment and control their own operations. The group is calling for written opposition to Ohio legislators.

Mark says: If you run a shop in Ohio, contact your state rep this week. OABA has the bill language and talking points ready.

Source: BodyShop Business →

INSURANCE

3. Florida insurer settles class action on storage and towing fees

A Florida insurance company agreed to settle a class action suit by paying storage charges, towing fees, and title and tag transfer costs to affected policyholders. The settlement signals growing pressure on insurers over storage fee disputes. Similar litigation is active in Oklahoma, where the Farm Bureau filed an amicus brief supporting a storage fee cap law.

Mark says: Document every storage day and towing invoice. You may need it in a fee dispute or denial appeal.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

OEM

4. BMW expands ADAS suite across X3, i3, X5, and 7 Series

BMW has announced expanded driver assistance and active safety systems now available on the new X3, i3, X5, and 7 Series models, with data collection in the EU for ongoing safety upgrades. The rollout signals that more BMW models are getting ADAS as standard, which means more recalibration work for shops seeing these vehicles. BMW also published research on OEM data privacy policies.

Mark says: Add these BMW models to your ADAS intake checklist and pull the latest calibration bulletin before the first one rolls in.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

TRAINING

5. SEMA wins FIA Innovation Challenge for ADAS research

SEMA was named FIA Innovation Challenge 2026 champion for its ADAS research initiative, which helps the automotive industry understand how vehicle modifications interact with ADAS systems. The recognition underscores the industry focus on ADAS accuracy and the growing need for shops to understand modification impacts on sensor and camera performance.

Mark says: Check SEMA's ADAS research library for technical bulletins on modifications and sensor coverage.
