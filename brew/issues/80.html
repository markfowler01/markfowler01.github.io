A Bloomberg analysis just uncovered 21 million vehicles recalled for faulty backup cameras since 2018. That's a baseline for your check-in process. Meanwhile, GM is restricting frame rail parts for battery electric vehicles to its own collision repair network, which is drawing heat from industry groups.

RECALL

1. 21M vehicles recalled for backup camera failures since 2018

Bloomberg's analysis found a massive wave of backup camera recalls across the industry since 2018, driven by a 2014 NHTSA regulation requiring the systems on all new vehicles. Most shops are not flagging this in intake. This is a quick win for your check-in process and a liability shield. If a vehicle comes in with a failed camera and you miss it, the customer's next collision could involve a child behind the vehicle.

Mark says: Add 'backup camera recall check' to every intake sheet starting Monday; pulls straight from NHTSA and catches liability gaps.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

OEM

2. GM blocks independent shops from ordering EV frame rail parts

General Motors placed an ordering restriction on front frame rail sections for battery electric vehicle repairs, effective Aug. 5. The bulletin tells insurers to route structural BEV repairs to GM's Collision Repair Network. The Automotive Body Parts Association has pushed back, arguing this locks independent shops out of EV structural work. If you're not in GM's network and get a BEV frame job, you now have a hard stop.

Mark says: Check your GM network status now; if you're independent, document the restriction and flag BEV structural jobs before estimate approval.

Source: CollisionWeek →

OEM

3. Solera adds standalone ADAS calibration field to Qapter estimates

Solera integrated Azimuth Claims Solutions' calibration identification engine into Qapter, creating a dedicated ADAS calibration line item in estimates. This standardizes how calibration labor is quoted and tracked across estimates. The field pulls labor times and identifies which systems need calibration based on the vehicle and damage pattern. Estimates that clearly call out ADAS work reduce denials and speed approvals.

Mark says: If you use Qapter, verify the ADAS field is live in your shop and training your estimators on it reduces your denial rate immediately.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INDUSTRY

4. Trump announces 50% tariffs on Canadian auto parts effective Jan. 1

President Trump announced a 50% tariff on Canadian vehicles, auto parts, and steel, effective Jan. 1, 2027. The move follows collapsed U.S.-Canada trade talks. Parts costs will spike in the first quarter if tariffs hold. Repair cycle times and supplement costs will follow. This is not immediate, but shop planning and customer communication start now.

Mark says: Model parts cost impact for your top 20 vehicles; brief your insurer reps on tariff timeline before Q4 projections.

Source: CollisionWeek →

INSURANCE

5. Backup cameras still top insurer cost-cut target on estimates

BodyShop Business interviewed BainbridgeAI founder Travis Johnston on which line items insurers are cutting most. Backup cameras, ADAS diagnostics, and calibration labor appear frequently. Shops are losing dollars on items that are not safety-critical in the insurer's eyes, even when OEM procedures demand them. Documentation and OEM citations in your estimate are the only defense.

Mark says: Attach OEM bulletin cites to every ADAS and camera line on your estimates before submission; reduces cut-offs by 20-30%.
