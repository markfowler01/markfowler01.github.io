RECALL

1. 11th death linked to counterfeit airbag; NHTSA enforcement ongoing

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration confirmed another fatality caused by a counterfeit airbag part the agency banned earlier in 2026. The tally continues to climb as investigators track distribution and install chains. This matters: shops that source parts from unfamiliar suppliers or see suspiciously low airbag pricing need to verify OEM compliance and documentation before install.

Mark says: Audit your airbag vendor paperwork this week. One bad install ends a driver's life and your shop's license.

RECALL

2. Park-outside recalls tied to vehicle tech, not just EV batteries

A Washington Post investigation found that the uptick in park-outside recalls stems from broad vehicle technology issues, including ADAS and infotainment systems, not EVs alone. Federal authorities are flagging software glitches, sensor malfunctions, and autonomous feature failures as drivers. Shops should expect these vehicles to sit in the lot longer while OEM updates deploy.

Mark says: Flag ADAS and infotainment modules in incoming vehicle checks. Consult NHTSA recall portal before committing to timeline.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

INDUSTRY

3. Bipartisan bill targets crash-for-cash fraud as federal crime

Congress has introduced legislation to make motor vehicle collision fraud a federal offense, aiming to improve driver safety and reduce insurance costs. The bill targets organized crash schemes and staged accidents. Passage would give federal authorities new tools to pursue fraud rings and potentially reduce false claims pressure on shops.

Mark says: Document every intake photo and customer statement. Federal enforcement will favor shops with clean, thorough collision records.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

INSURANCE

4. Farmers alerts customers on ADAS repair complexity and cost

Farmers Insurance is informing policyholders that new safety technology in vehicles, particularly ADAS, may extend repair timelines and increase costs. The carrier is setting expectations upfront with consumers on repair complexity. Shops should expect more policyholder questions and may face tighter supplement scrutiny as insurers defend their cost estimates.

Mark says: When Farmers appraises ADAS work, cite OEM time studies and calibration labor in every supplement before submission.

Source: BodyShop Business →

INDUSTRY

5. California suspends third LA repair shop over fraudulent safety inspections

The California Bureau of Automotive Repair suspended another Los Angeles repair dealer for issuing fraudulent safety inspections on rebuilt total-loss vehicles. The license suspension signals stepped-up enforcement on rebuilt-title compliance. Shops handling rebuilt vehicles or total-loss reentry work must maintain clean documentation and follow all BAR inspection protocols.

Mark says: If you touch a rebuilt title, triple-check your BAR paperwork and keep records for five years. Regulators are watching.
