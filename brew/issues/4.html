Parts pricing flips; ADAS gets industry focus
Monday, May 11 · #4

OEM parts are climbing in price while aftermarket stays steady—a shift worth watching on your supplement line. Meanwhile, the industry just launched two new ADAS-focused initiatives to help shops navigate risk and opportunity.

INSURANCE

1. OEM parts spike; aftermarket holds steady in Q1

OEM new parts saw the biggest price jump in the latest quarter, while recycled and aftermarket parts remained flat. This matters because it'll push more estimates toward alternative parts and harder negotiations with insurers who want to cap costs. PartsTrader's Spring 2026 data shows the gap widening—expect more supplement pushback from carriers on OEM component replacements.

Source: PartsTrader / SCRS →

TRAINING

2. Two new ADAS initiatives launch across industry

The Collision Industry Conference and SCRS are rolling out programs focused on ADAS risks and opportunities for shops. Both aim to build community knowledge around calibration requirements, liability exposure, and business growth in an increasingly systems-heavy vehicle landscape. This is the industry signaling that ADAS competency is now table-stakes.

Source: Auto Body News / SCRS →

INDUSTRY

3. Driven Brands faces $18/share buyout bid from activist

ADW Capital Management proposed acquiring Driven Brands at $18 per share—a 41% premium over the stock price at the time of the offer. The move signals consolidation pressure in the multi-brand operator space. If the deal closes, it could reshape service pricing, calibration requirements, and supply chains for shops that partner with Driven's banner companies.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

INDUSTRY

4. CCC reports strong Q1; doubles down on AI

CCC Intelligent Solutions posted 12% revenue growth to $281.3M in Q1, with leadership emphasizing AI expansion across estimating and claims workflows. Insurers are leaning hard into CCC's automation tools, which means shops need to stay aligned with whatever data formats and supplement protocols those carriers demand. More AI adoption = more friction points for independents without integration.

Source: Collision Repair Magazine / Repairer Driven News →

INDUSTRY

5. LKQ: alternative parts hit 40% utilization in Q1

LKQ reported alternative parts utilization (recycled, refurbished, aftermarket) reached an all-time high of 40% in the first quarter. This reflects both carrier push-back on OEM costs and shops' pragmatic embrace of non-OEM solutions. For calibration shops, this means more vehicles with non-OEM sensors and cameras—verify part provenance and specs before you dial in ADAS.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

Two new ADAS programs launch and parts pricing shifts this week. Make sure your team has current OEM calibration specs and alternative-parts compatibility guides.
