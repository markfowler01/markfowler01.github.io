Honda and Hyundai both dropped recalls this week that will land on your intake. We also pulled Ford's latest SRS repair guidance and a reminder on what GEICO's claim data shows about severity.

RECALL

1. Honda recalls 600K+ US vehicles for steering assist software failure

Honda is recalling over 600,000 vehicles over a software issue in the steering assist system. This is not a minor calibration job. Affected vehicles will need diagnosis and likely software updates or component replacement. Your intake desk should flag these at check-in so you can plan labor and parts accordingly.

Mark says: Confirm Honda steering assist on every Honda intake this week. High volume incoming; coordinate with your parts supplier on availability now.

Source: Reuters →

RECALL

2. Hyundai airbag software glitch disables restraint system, 37,901 recalled

Hyundai has recalled 37,901 vehicles for a software malfunction that disables the airbag entirely. This is a safety-critical defect. Customers will need to bring vehicles in for diagnostic and likely ECU reprogramming. Run a recall lookup on every Hyundai at check-in this week.

Mark says: Add Hyundai airbag recall to your standard intake checklist. If you see one in the lot, isolate it and don't release until the OEM fix is confirmed.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

TSB

3. Ford On Target: SRS repowering procedures for Bronco and other models

Ford's latest On Target bulletin covers supplemental restraint system (SRS) repowering procedures, including detailed steps for the Bronco platform. This is procedural guidance for any shop working on Ford SRS repairs. The bulletin walks through pre-repair diagnostics and post-repair verification. Download and add to your calibration checklist before the next Ford SRS job lands.

Mark says: Pull Ford's On Target SRS bulletin now and attach it to every Ford SRS supplement estimate you write going forward.

Source: Repairer Driven News →

INSURANCE

4. GEICO Q3: Claim severity climbs as collision frequencies rise 2-4%

GEICO reported property damage and collision claim frequency up 2 to 4 percent through nine months of 2026, with severity increasing 0 to 3 percent. Underwriting earnings fell 44.6 percent year-over-year. This data signals that insurers are tightening. Higher severity means shops need tighter estimating and documentation to defend supplement requests.

Mark says: Insurers feel the squeeze now. Document calibration work with OEM cites; don't rely on generic labor guides alone when writing supplements.

Source: CollisionWeek →

OEM

5. AirPro + Revv combine ADAS workflow from ID through reimbursement

AirPro and Revv (now combined) launched four new products at SEMA that integrate the full ADAS cycle into one workflow: vehicle ID, calibration planning, procedure tracking, and reimbursement coding. The goal is to reduce friction between the bay and billing. If you're wrestling with ADAS reimbursement denials, this is worth a look.

Mark says: Review the new AirPro+Revv suite if ADAS billing is costing you supplements or appeals. Connected workflows can close the gap between bay time and insurer approval.
