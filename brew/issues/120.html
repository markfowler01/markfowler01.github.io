OEM

1. CCC embeds five vendors' calibration recs into estimates

CCC Intelligent Solutions launched Calibration Guidelines, which pulls recommendations from Autel, Hunter, Bosch, Snap-on, and others directly into CCC ONE and insurer workflows. No extra charge. This means your cal work shows up in the estimate before the job starts, not after. Eliminates the supplement fight.

Mark says: Train your front desk to flag every CCC estimate that lists calibration. Attach the OEM bulletin to every supplement before it gets denied.

2. Toyota recall: 105,800 US vehicles over fire risk

Toyota issued a recall for approximately 105,800 US vehicles due to fire risk. NHTSA has the details. Depending on the model years and systems involved, some of these repairs may trigger ADAS recalibration. Check your customer intake this week for Toyota and pull the recall lookup.

Mark says: Pull the NHTSA recall details today. If it touches fuel or electrical, ADAS sensors may have been repositioned. Add cal to your scope now.

🛠 Operator's Corner
For the folks who run a calibration business

When a Carrier Denies Your Calibration

Most denials come with a stated reason. Read it carefully, then pull the OEM position statement that covers that exact vehicle and system. Your reply should be short and direct: the OEM says calibration is required after this repair, here is the document. Carriers move faster on a focused response than a long argument. Keep a simple log of which carriers deny what so you stop starting from scratch every time.
