🎯 Carrier of the Week
State Farm

State Farm is still cutting pre-scans. Here's the cite that stops it.

What they're cutting
  • Denying pre-scan as 'not related to loss' or 'included in diagnostic time'
  • Cutting post-scan line items as duplicative when calibration is already on the estimate
  • Flagging sublet calibration invoices as 'not verified' and holding or reducing payment
Rebuttal that's flipping it
Pre- and post-scans are separate, documented procedures required by virtually every OEM position statement, including Honda, Toyota, and Hyundai, whose vehicles are hitting shops right now with active recall and ADAS exposure. I-CAR's position is unambiguous: scanning is not diagnostic overlap, it is a required step in the repair process, and each scan serves a distinct purpose.

Three OEM recalls land this week (Honda Takata, Ram ABS, Toyota airbag), Nissan's diagnostic system is down after a cyber event, and Hyundai just killed its non-OEM parts approval. Here's what hits your intake this Monday.

RECALL

1. Honda Takata inflator, Ram ABS, Toyota airbag recalls hit shops

Three fresh recalls crossed the wire. Honda has a Takata inflator recall, Ram issued an ABS software problem, and Toyota has a passenger airbag issue on the books. Check your intake system against these three VINs this week before estimates go out. Each has specific model years and production dates.

Mark says: Post these three recalls at your counter and add them to your pre-intake checklist today.

Source: Autobody News / NHTSA →

OEM

2. Nissan diagnostic system offline after cybersecurity incident

Nissan shut down its vehicle diagnostic system late last week after a cybersecurity event. The system is used by repairers to access Nissan vehicle data for calibration and repair work. No timeline given for restoration. Contact your Nissan rep for interim procedures and expected return date.

Mark says: Call your Nissan account contact now. Know the workaround procedure before a Nissan calibration job lands Monday.

Source: Repairer Driven News / SCRS →

OEM

3. Hyundai kills non-OEM parts approval in collision repair

Hyundai revised its parts policy on OEM1Stop. The new language no longer approves use of non-OEM or competitive parts in collision repair. This tightens language from prior guidance. Update your estimate workflows and talk to suppliers about Hyundai jobs going forward.

Mark says: Pull the OEM1Stop statement and brief your parts manager and estimators on Hyundai parts restrictions today.

Source: Repairer Driven News / SCRS →

TRAINING

4. AAA finds limits in intersection automatic emergency braking

AAA tested four 2026 model-year vehicles with intersection AEB and found real performance gaps in certain crash scenarios. These systems are still learning. Some vehicles did not stop or reacted slowly in left-turn and crossing-path situations. Useful data for your team on what these systems can and cannot do in the real world.

Mark says: File this AAA report and use it when customers ask if their new car's AEB will prevent accidents-it won't, always.

Source: Repairer Driven News / SCRS →

OEM

5. US proposes 2031 deadline for automatic emergency braking mandate

The federal government proposed extending the automaker deadline to install automatic emergency braking systems to 2031. This is a regulatory push, not yet final rule. AEB adoption will accelerate as the deadline nears, which means more AEB calibration work coming to shops in the next 18 to 36 months.

Mark says: More AEB systems coming to inventory. Train your team on AEB calibration procedures now before the rush hits.

Source: Reuters →
