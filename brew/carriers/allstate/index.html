The 5 most common Allstate calibration denials

Pattern #1 · Most Common

"Calibration not required per our internal labor database"

"Calibration line item not authorized — does not appear in our reference labor guide for this repair operation."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Allstate's "reference labor guide" is not the OEM's repair procedure. The two are different documents. The OEM repair procedure is the controlling authority for what's required after a given repair operation — not the carrier's internal database. Cite the manufacturer document by name and page number. Their internal guide doesn't override OEM specifications.

Pattern #2 · Recurring

"Aftermarket part is equivalent and OEM-required calibration is unnecessary"

"Replace forward-facing camera with aftermarket equivalent. Standard calibration sufficient."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Most OEMs (Honda, Toyota, GM, Subaru) explicitly require OEM hardware and OEM procedure for any ADAS component that integrates with the vehicle's safety architecture. Pull the OEM ADAS position statement from the cheat sheet — it's almost never ambiguous. "Aftermarket equivalent" is the adjuster's opinion, not the manufacturer's.

Pattern #3 · Documentation Push

"Insufficient justification — please resubmit with additional documentation"

"Calibration line item paused pending additional documentation supporting necessity."

→ The OEM-cited flip

This is a stall, not a denial. The play is to come back overwhelming. Attach the OEM repair procedure with the page citation, the pre-scan report, the post-scan report, the calibration certification, and photos of the target setup with VIN visible. Adjusters can't push back on a documented procedure-based requirement without escalating to a position they can't defend in writing.

Pattern #4 · Time Cut

"Labor time exceeds standard for this operation"

"Reducing calibration labor from 1.5 hours to 0.7 hours per our standard time guide."

→ The OEM-cited flip

There is no industry "standard time" for OEM-procedure ADAS calibration. Each calibration requires the time specified by the OEM — including target setup, lighting verification, surface leveling, and the actual scan tool sequence. Cite the OEM procedure's quoted time and the actual time documented on your sublet invoice. If they cannot produce the labor guide they're referencing, the labor stands.

Pattern #5 · Bundled Charge

"Pre-scan and post-scan included in standard diagnostic time"

"Pre/post scan operations are part of the diagnostic charge already authorized — no separate line item."

→ The OEM-cited flip

OEM-required pre-repair and post-repair scans are distinct operations from "diagnostic time", and are explicitly called out as separate, required procedures in the manufacturer repair documentation. Honda, Toyota, and GM all have specific position statements on this. Cite the position statement directly — they exist precisely because of this denial pattern.

Build the paper wall before the fight starts

Shops winning Allstate disputes consistently document before the work starts — not after the denial.

1. OEM repair procedure printout stapled to the RO before work begins
2. Pre-scan and post-scan reports, date-stamped
3. Calibration line items separated from labor, with the OEM procedure citation attached
4. Photos of target setup with VIN visible
5. Written log of any verbal Allstate pushback (date, adjuster name, claim number)
