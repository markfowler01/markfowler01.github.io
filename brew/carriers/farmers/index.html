The 5 most common Farmers calibration denials

Pattern #1 · Most Common

"Pre-scan and post-scan included in diagnostic time"

"Pre/post scan operations are part of authorized diagnostic charge. No separate line item required."

→ The OEM-cited flip

OEM-required pre-repair and post-repair scans are distinct operations from diagnostic time. Honda, Toyota, GM, Subaru, and Ford have explicit position statements treating pre/post scans as required, separately-billable operations. Cite the OEM position statement directly — these documents exist precisely because of this denial pattern. I-CAR RTS also positions pre/post scans as distinct operations.

Pattern #2 · Bundling

"Calibration is part of R&I labor"

"Forward camera calibration considered included in bumper R&I labor operation."

→ The OEM-cited flip

No published OEM labor guide bundles ADAS calibration into R&I operations. R&I covers mechanical removal and reinstallation; calibration is a separate scan-tool-driven procedure with its own target setup, lighting requirements, and OEM-specified time. Cite the OEM repair procedure showing calibration as a distinct step with its own labor time. The procedure document is the authority — not the carrier's bundling.

Pattern #3 · Time Cut

"Time exceeds standard for this calibration"

"Reducing calibration labor from 1.2 hours to 0.5 hours per Farmers labor standard."

→ The OEM-cited flip

There is no published industry "standard" for OEM-procedure ADAS calibration time. Each procedure has manufacturer-specified equipment, target setup, surface leveling, lighting verification, and scan tool steps. Document the OEM procedure's quoted time and the actual recorded time on your sublet invoice. If Farmers cannot produce the labor standard they're citing, the labor time stands.

Pattern #4 · Duplicate Operations

"Multiple line items represent same operation"

"Forward camera calibration and radar calibration submitted as separate items. Considered duplicate. Authorizing one."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Forward camera and radar are physically distinct sensors with distinct OEM calibration procedures, distinct equipment requirements, and distinct labor times. They're not interchangeable. Document each procedure separately with its own OEM citation, target setup photos, and scan tool report. The carrier doesn't get to declare two different sensors "the same operation."

Pattern #5 · Aftermarket Push

"OEM hardware not required — aftermarket equivalent acceptable"

"Replace sensor with aftermarket equivalent. Standard calibration applies."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Most major OEMs (Honda, Toyota, Subaru, GM) have explicit position statements requiring OEM hardware for safety-system components. Aftermarket sensors often cannot be calibrated to OEM tolerances. Pull the OEM ADAS hardware position statement from the cheat sheet and cite it directly. The position is the manufacturer's, not the shop's.

Build the paper wall before the fight starts

Farmers disputes turn on itemization. Bundle nothing. Document everything as distinct.

1. OEM repair procedure printout with each calibration step distinguished
2. Pre-scan and post-scan reports as separate, date-stamped line items
3. Each calibration as its own line, with its own OEM procedure citation
4. Photos of target setup for EACH calibration with VIN visible
5. Written narrative if multiple ADAS systems were calibrated — explaining the distinct procedures
