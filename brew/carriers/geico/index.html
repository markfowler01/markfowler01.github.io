Geico's playbook: aftermarket parts and internal "guidelines."
OEM citations beat both.

Geico's denial pattern leans heavily on pushing aftermarket parts as "industry standard equivalent," citing internal Geico-approved guidelines instead of OEM procedures, and reducing sublet calibration to internally-determined "prevailing rates." Below are the most common Geico denial patterns and the OEM citations that flip them.

The 5 most common Geico calibration denials

Pattern #1 · Most Common

"Aftermarket part is industry standard equivalent"

"Aftermarket forward camera satisfies repair specification. OEM premium not justified."

→ The OEM-cited flip

"Industry standard equivalent" is the carrier's opinion, not the manufacturer's. Honda, Toyota, GM, Subaru, and Ford all have explicit position statements requiring OEM hardware for ADAS components that integrate with the vehicle's safety architecture. Pull the OEM ADAS position statement from the cheat sheet and cite it. The manufacturer is the authority on what's equivalent — not Geico.

Pattern #2 · Internal Guideline

"Calibration not required per Geico-approved repair guidelines"

"This calibration is not authorized per Geico's repair authorization guidelines."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Geico's internal repair guidelines are not OEM repair procedures. They do not override manufacturer documentation. The OEM repair procedure governs what's required for that specific vehicle. Cite the manufacturer document by name and page, and request the source citation behind Geico's "approved repair guideline" — it usually doesn't exist as a publishable document, just as an internal flag in their system.

Pattern #3 · Bundled Calibration

"Aftermarket sensor includes pre-calibration — separate calibration unnecessary"

"Aftermarket camera assembly is pre-programmed. Additional in-vehicle calibration not required."

→ The OEM-cited flip

No ADAS forward camera works correctly without in-vehicle, vehicle-specific calibration. Even a brand-new OEM sensor still requires static or dynamic calibration to align with the specific vehicle's geometry, windshield angle, and target reference. The OEM procedure explicitly specifies the in-vehicle calibration as a required post-install step. Cite the procedure. An "in-the-box pre-calibration" claim by the parts supplier doesn't satisfy the manufacturer's procedure.

Pattern #4 · Rate Cap

"Reducing sublet calibration to prevailing rate"

"Sublet calibration cost exceeds Geico-determined prevailing rate. Reduced to $X."

→ The OEM-cited flip

"Prevailing rate" with no source citation isn't a benchmark — it's a flag. Each calibration requires OEM-specified equipment, target setup, lighting verification, and labor time that vary by make and model. Document the OEM procedure's specified equipment and time, attach the sublet invoice from a manufacturer-recognized provider, and request Geico's market survey that supports their "prevailing rate." If they can't produce one, the invoice stands.

Pattern #5 · Scope Limit

"Position statement does not apply to this repair scope"

"OEM position statement referenced applies only to full sensor replacement, not the operation performed."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Most OEM position statements are explicit about triggering operations — not just replacement. Honda, Toyota, and GM ADAS position statements specifically list R&I, windshield replacement, suspension work, and bumper R&R as triggering operations independent of sensor replacement. Cite the trigger language directly from the position statement. The carrier is reading the document selectively; you read it accurately.

Build the paper wall before the fight starts

Geico disputes turn on OEM authority vs internal "guidelines." Bring more OEM, not more argument.

1. OEM repair procedure printout with the calibration trigger highlighted
2. OEM ADAS position statement on hardware/parts requirements
3. Pre-scan and post-scan reports, date-stamped
4. Calibration line items separated with OEM procedure citations
5. Photos of target setup with VIN visible
6. Written request for Geico to produce the source documentation behind their "guideline" or "prevailing rate"
