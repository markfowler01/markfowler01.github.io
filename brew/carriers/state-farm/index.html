The 5 most common State Farm calibration denials

These are the patterns we hear from shops weekly. Each one has a known OEM-cited counter — none of them have to stick.

Pattern #1 · Most Common

"Calibration is included in the standard repair labor"

"Pre-scan / forward camera calibration not authorized — considered included in standard bumper R&R labor."

→ The OEM-cited flip

No OEM publishes a labor guide that includes calibration in mechanical R&R operations. Cite the manufacturer's repair procedure (Toyota TIS, Honda Body Repair News, GM SI, Ford OEM1Stop, etc.) showing the calibration as a separate, required, and time-quoted operation. The procedure document is your evidence — not your opinion.

Pattern #2 · Recurring

"Aftermarket camera/sensor is acceptable"

"Replace forward-facing camera with available aftermarket equivalent. OEM not required."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Most major OEMs (Honda, Toyota, GM, Subaru) have explicit position statements requiring OEM ADAS hardware for any system that integrates with the vehicle's safety architecture. Look up the OEM's ADAS position statement on our cheat sheet and cite it directly. The statement isn't ambiguous.

Pattern #3 · Documentation Push

"Calibration not necessary — no fault codes present"

"Post-scan shows no DTCs. Calibration not required."

→ The OEM-cited flip

DTC-free does NOT mean calibration-not-needed. OEM repair procedures specify calibration as a triggered event — based on R&R of certain components, not on whether codes appear. The Pre-Collision System or radar can be perfectly "code-free" while being misaligned by 0.5° and not flagging anything until a real-world incident. Cite the OEM's procedure-trigger language.

Pattern #4 · Sublet Squeeze

"Sublet calibration cost is excessive"

"Sublet calibration invoice exceeds prevailing rate. Reducing to $X."

→ The OEM-cited flip

There is no published "prevailing rate" for OEM-procedure ADAS calibration. Each calibration has different equipment, target, and time requirements per the OEM. Document the time and equipment the OEM procedure specifies, attach the sublet invoice, and request a market survey. If they can't produce one, the rate stands.

Pattern #5 · Mid-Repair Cut

"Authorization withdrawn after job started"

"Re-evaluation: calibration line item removed from authorization."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Carriers cannot retroactively de-authorize a calibration that was procedurally required by the OEM repair documentation in place when the job started. Document the original authorization, attach the OEM repair procedure with the calibration requirement, and add the GM/UMich crash study (ADAS reduces injury crashes by up to 57%) as the public-safety backbone. Liability tilts toward the carrier when calibration is undocumented and an incident occurs post-repair.

Build the paper wall before the fight starts

The shops that win State Farm disputes consistently are not the loudest. They're the ones with documentation already attached when the supplement gets reviewed:

1. OEM repair procedure printout stapled to the RO before work starts
2. Pre-scan and post-scan reports, date-stamped
3. Calibration line items separated from labor, with the OEM procedure citation attached
4. Photos of target setup with the VIN visible
5. Written log of any verbal carrier pushback (date, adjuster name, claim number)
