The 5 most common Liberty Mutual calibration denials

Pattern #1 · Most Common

"Damage to ADAS hardware not documented — calibration not required"

"Photos do not show visible damage to forward camera or sensor assembly. Calibration line item not authorized."

→ The OEM-cited flip

OEM calibration triggers are not based on visible damage. They're based on operations performed — bumper R&I, windshield replacement, suspension work, sensor relocation. The manufacturer specifies the trigger. Cite the OEM procedure showing the operation that was performed is itself a calibration trigger, independent of any visible damage assessment.

Pattern #2 · Second Opinion

"Independent review required before authorizing calibration"

"Calibration line item under review pending independent assessment. Authorization pending."

→ The OEM-cited flip

"Independent assessment" usually means another body shop estimate — not an OEM-procedure review. The OEM repair procedure IS the controlling document. Send the OEM procedure citation, the pre-scan and post-scan reports, and the calibration certification from a manufacturer-recognized scan tool, and request that the "independent reviewer" produce contradicting OEM documentation. Spoiler: they can't.

Pattern #3 · Multi-Cal Cut

"Multiple ADAS calibrations on single job — authorizing one"

"Forward camera, radar, and surround-view calibrations submitted as separate line items. Authorizing forward camera only."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Each ADAS subsystem has its own calibration procedure, equipment, target setup, and OEM-specified time requirement. They are not interchangeable or bundleable. Document each calibration's distinct procedure, equipment, and time. A radar calibration cannot serve as a surround-view calibration — different sensors, different procedures, different OEM citations. Itemize and cite each.

Pattern #4 · Severity Push

"Calibration cost not supported by claim severity"

"Total claim value does not support calibration line items at submitted amount."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Calibration requirements are procedure-based, not severity-based. The OEM does not specify "skip calibration on low-severity claims." Cite the procedure trigger and the GM-funded University of Michigan crash study (ADAS reduces injury crashes by up to 57%). The carrier's "severity" rationale doesn't override the manufacturer's safety procedure.

Pattern #5 · Hidden Damage

"Pre-collision photos do not show damage in sensor area"

"Pre-loss photos and damage estimate do not support sensor recalibration scope."

→ The OEM-cited flip

Pre-collision photos can't show what was disturbed during repair. Document the repair-operation chain: collision damage → repair operation → component disturbance → OEM-specified calibration trigger. Even a clean exterior can hide bracket misalignment, sensor offset, or windshield seal compression that the OEM procedure flags as a calibration trigger. The OEM procedure governs, not the carrier's photo interpretation.

Build the paper wall before the fight starts

Liberty Mutual disputes turn on the trigger chain. Document the operation-to-calibration linkage in writing.

1. OEM repair procedure printout, with the calibration trigger highlighted
2. Pre-scan and post-scan reports, date-stamped
3. Calibration line items separated, each with its own OEM procedure citation
4. Photos of target setup with VIN visible
5. Written narrative: collision → operation → component disturbance → calibration trigger
