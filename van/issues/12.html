Passed the cal. Pulled on the freeway a week later.
From the Van · Issue #12

A windshield R&R on an F-150 passed its camera calibration clean. Owner picked it up. A week later the truck is back because lane centering tugs to the right on a straight road.

Here's the trap. The IPMA camera behind the glass is picky about what it looks through. Wrong glass variant, a tint shade band in the camera window, or the wrong bracket, and the system will still complete a static calibration. It reads the targets in the shop fine.

Then the truck gets on the freeway. The camera is looking through glass it was never speced for, the image is off just enough, and the lane keep assist starts pulling. No code. The light stays off. Just a truck that fights the driver.

The glass that went in was aftermarket. It fit. It sealed. It was not the variant Ford calls out for a camera-equipped windshield.

Here's what that means for you: a calibration that passes on paper is not proof the glass behind it is right. The cal confirms aim. It does not confirm you installed OE-correct glass with the right bracket and clear camera window.

Ask your cal shop this week: "Did you verify the windshield variant and the camera window is clear before you ran the calibration?"

Skip that question and the comeback is yours. The glass vendor billed the shop, the shop billed the customer, and now the truck is back in your bay on your dime with a drift nobody logged.

The van has seen a lot of glass that fit and still lied.
