New bumper beam, same old radar angle
From the Van · Issue #13

Front hit on a Tiguan. Bumper beam bent, replaced. Car drove out. Adaptive cruise worked in the lot.

Then it came back a week later. Owner said it brakes for cars in the next lane on the freeway.

Here is the trap. On a lot of VW and Audi, the front radar does not bolt to the body. It rides on the bumper beam itself, on a bracket that is part of the crash structure. Replace the beam, and the radar goes back on at whatever angle the new bracket gives you.

No code. No lamp. The radar talks fine. It is just aimed a few degrees off center, reading the guardrail or the next lane as a threat.

That is not a calibration you can skip because the system "seems fine." A radar that is pointed wrong will pass its own self-check and still phantom-brake at 65. The angle is the whole job.

Here's what that means for you: any front structural part on a radar car can move the radar's aim, even if nobody touched the radar. The part swap IS the trigger.

Ask your cal shop this week: "If we replaced a bumper beam or front structure, does the front radar need a re-aim even with no codes?" If they say the radar is fine because it powers up, that is the wrong answer.

Skip that re-aim and the car comes back phantom-braking on the freeway. That is a comeback with your name on the RO, and it is the kind that ends up in a claim file.

The van has opinions about radars that bolt to crash parts. None of them are warm.
