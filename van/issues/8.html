Solder cracks don't set a code. They set a comeback.
From the Van · Issue #8

A 2015 Odyssey came through after a windshield swap. Front camera recalibrated fine. No codes. Green across the board.

Then it came back a week later. Lane keep tugging the wrong way on the same stretch of I-5 every morning.

Here's the trap. On a lot of 2013 to 2016 Hondas, the forward camera has a solder joint problem inside the module. It doesn't fail clean. The calibration completes, the scan tool says pass, and everybody goes home happy.

Then heat cycles hit. The joint flexes. The camera starts throwing off aim intermittently, usually under sun load or a temperature swing. By the time it acts up, the car's out of your bay and the light isn't even on.

We caught this one by watching the live camera data during cal instead of trusting the pass screen. The values drifted while the module warmed. A clean pass that won't hold isn't a pass.

The van has seen enough 2015 Odysseys to have trust issues.

The takeaway: when you sublet a windshield calibration on a 2013 to 2016 Honda, ask whether they watched live camera data through the cal, not just the pass result. A module that solder-fails needs replacing, not recalibrating. That's the difference between one invoice and a comeback you eat.

A green screen is an opinion. Live data is a fact.
