The scan was clean. The car still wasn't.
From the Van · Issue #2

A Prius came in last week after a front-end hit. Radar sensor area, minor.

Pre-scan comes back clean. No DTCs. Nothing stored. The kind of scan report that makes everybody feel good about a quick cycle.

Here's the trap on these.

Toyota's pre-collision system on a lot of these years will not throw a code just because the radar got knocked out of aim. No fault light. No DTC. The car happily tells you everything is fine while the radar is pointed at the ground.

The only way you catch it is running the actual radar beam axis check inside the OEM procedure and looking at the live alignment data. Not the scan. The data behind the scan.

We pulled the numbers and the horizontal aim was off enough that the system would have braked late or not at all in a real event. Zero codes the whole time.

So the car that scanned perfect was the car most likely to come back.

Takeaway: When you sublet a calibration, a clean pre-scan is not proof the ADAS is aimed right. Ask whoever does your calibrations if they verified the radar axis data on the OEM procedure, not just the code list. "No codes" and "aimed correctly" are two different sentences.

The van has seen enough clean scans to stop trusting them on faith.
