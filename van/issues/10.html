Aftermarket windshield, clean install, no cal in sight
From the Van · Issue #10

A Subaru Outback came through with a fresh windshield. Glass looked perfect. No cracks, no leaks, clean install.

The car had EyeSight, which lives on the two cameras up at the top of the windshield. Touch that glass, you touch the cameras.

Subaru is clear on this. Any windshield R&R on an EyeSight car means a full static calibration. Not optional. Not dynamic-only. Static, in a controlled bay, with the targets set to spec.

Here's the trap. The shop that put the glass in used an aftermarket windshield. When we set up for the static cal, the car wouldn't complete it. The camera bracket geometry on that aftermarket glass was off just enough that EyeSight couldn't confirm its own aim.

Subaru cameras are picky about the glass they look through. Wrong optical zone, wrong bracket angle, and the system either fails the cal or passes and drifts later.

We flagged it, the shop sourced OE glass, and the second cal completed clean.

Here's what that means for you: an EyeSight car with aftermarket glass is a comeback waiting to happen. The install can be flawless and the cal still won't pass. That's a delay you want to catch on the estimate, not the day the customer picks up.

Ask your cal shop this week: "On an EyeSight Subaru, does the glass we spec actually pass a static calibration, or are we finding out at the target board?"

The van has seen a lot of perfect-looking windshields lately. It remains unimpressed.
