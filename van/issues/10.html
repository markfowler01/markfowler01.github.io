FCW light on, zero codes, and the fix wasn't the camera.
From the Van · Issue #10

A Grand Cherokee L came in with a forward collision warning light on. No crash. No front-end work. Just a dash light that showed up out of nowhere.

The shop that sent it over figured the camera got knocked out of aim. Reasonable guess. It was wrong.

Here's the trap. This generation Grand Cherokee will throw an FCW and forward-facing camera complaint after a radio or infotainment software update. The module updates, the network handshake gets sloppy, and the car lights up like something's broken. Pull codes and you get nothing that points you anywhere useful. Just a lamp and a bad feeling.

The fix isn't a new camera. It isn't a static calibration in your bay. It's getting the modules back on the same software page and clearing the fault the right way, in the right order. Throw a camera at it and you've spent the customer's money on a part that was never the problem.

This is the kind of thing that turns into a comeback three days later when the light comes back on in the customer's driveway.

The van has seen enough "no codes but the light's on" cars to have a personal opinion about them. It keeps that opinion to itself.

Takeaway: If a Jeep shows up with an ADAS light after any infotainment or radio work, don't let anyone order a camera before the software state gets checked. Ask your calibration partner whether a module update is what actually kicked the light on. That question saves you a part and a comeback.
