Three coats of paint, one radar that couldn't see
From the Van · Issue #11

A Rogue came in for a dynamic radar cal after a front hit. The bumper cover looked great. Fresh paint, clean lines, nice work.

The cal would not finish.

No bent bracket. No DTC screaming at us. The radar sat behind the bumper cover trying to complete its drive cycle and just kept timing out. Car after car on the same road that finishes fine.

Here's the trap. The front radar on a lot of these fires straight through the bumper cover. That plastic is part of the optical path. When a cover gets primed, based, cleared, and then a shop hits it again to bury a blend, you can stack up way more film thickness than the radar was built to see through.

The radar isn't broken. It's looking through a foggy window. The signal comes back weak and the module never trusts it enough to say "done."

We measured film build, the painter re-shot the cover to spec, and the dynamic cal completed on the first pass.

Here's what that means for you. A dynamic cal that times out over and over usually isn't a bad module. Before anyone talks about replacing a radar, somebody should be asking what's in front of it.

Ask your cal shop this week: "If a front radar dynamic cal keeps timing out, are you checking paint film thickness on the bumper cover before you condemn the radar?"

Guess wrong and you eat a radar you didn't need, the car sits another week waiting on the part, and the comeback lands on your RO. The van has seen a lot of good paint jobs that a radar politely refused to look through.
