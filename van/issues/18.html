Phantom beeps, no code, and a repaint nobody measured
From the Van · Issue #18

A Ram 1500 came in beeping at nothing.

Backup, pull into a slot, beeps like something's two feet off the bumper. Nothing there. Shop pulled codes. Clean. No DTC, no lamp, no stored history.

So it looks like a bad sensor. That's where money starts walking out the door.

Here's what actually happened. The rear bumper cover got repaired and repainted after a tap. Looks great. But the ultrasonic park sensors sit behind that cover, and paint is not free weight to them. Too many coats, or a thick build over the sensor face, and the sound they send out comes back muddy. The sensor reads mud as an object.

No fault code, because nothing is electrically wrong. The sensor is doing its job. It's just listening through a wall of clear coat.

We measured paint thickness across the sensor zones. A couple ran way over spec. Stripped and refinished to the correct film build, beeps gone. No new parts.

Here's what that means for you. A phantom-beep comeback with no code is almost never a module. On a freshly painted bumper, it's film build until proven otherwise. Swapping a good sensor is parts cost, a comeback, and a customer who stops trusting the truck.

Ask your cal shop this week: "After a bumper repaint, are you checking paint thickness over the park sensor faces before you blame the sensor?"

The car maker gives you a max film build on those faces for a reason. Measure it. Document it. That's a supplement you get paid for instead of eating a return visit.

The van has strong feelings about phantom beeps. Mostly that they're never the module, and somebody always buys one anyway.
