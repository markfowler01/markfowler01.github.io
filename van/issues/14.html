Static passed. The car still fought the driver on a straight road.
From the Van · Issue #14

A CX-5 came back with a complaint the shop couldn't make sense of. Lane centering tugged the wheel on a dead straight road.

The static calibration had passed. Paperwork clean. Everybody moved on.

Here's the trap. During the windshield R&R, the forward camera bracket got re-glued to the glass slightly off-spec. Not by much. A couple millimeters and a hair of tilt.

Static calibration will happily pass that. The target board doesn't care where the camera thinks straight ahead is. It just confirms the camera sees the target. So it signs off green.

Then the car hits the road and the camera's idea of center is a little left of the actual center. Lane keep does exactly what you told it to and fights the driver.

The fix was not another calibration. The bracket had to come off and get re-bonded to OEM position first. Then recalibrate. Passed clean, drove straight.

Here's what that means for you. A passed static cal is not proof the camera is aimed right. It's proof the camera saw the target. If the bracket's off, you can pass the test and still hand back a car that steers wrong.

Ask your cal shop this week: "After the glass work, did you confirm the camera bracket is bonded to OEM position before you ran the calibration?"

Because a lane-keep pull is the kind of comeback that doesn't show as a code. The customer just knows the car fought them, and that one comes back on your RO, not the glass shop's.

The van has opinions about brackets that get re-glued by feel. None of them are warm.
