Bent bracket, clean scan, bad aim
From the Van · Issue #16

A rear corner got repaired on a Santa Fe. Clean work. The blind spot radar scanned green and "calibrated" without a fight.

Here's the trap. On Hyundai and Kia, the rear corner radar bolts to the quarter panel, not the bumper cover. When that quarter takes a hit, the bracket behind it can tweak a degree or two and nobody sees it. The bumper cover goes back on straight. The paint looks perfect.

The radar doesn't throw a code for being aimed wrong. It'll run its routine and report complete while it's staring two degrees off into the next lane. Static scan says everybody's happy.

The only way to catch it is to pull the bumper cover and verify the bracket is true before you trust the calibration. On these cars the car maker requires that cover off anyway, because they often need a hand adjustment. A tool that just reads "complete" isn't doing that part.

The van has seen a lot of green checkmarks that lied.

Here's what that means for you: a radar that "calibrated" on paper but reads the wrong lane is the kind of thing that comes back as a customer complaint, or worse, after a lane-change crash. The checkmark is not the proof.

Ask your cal shop this week: "On that Hyundai, did you pull the bumper cover and verify the corner radar bracket before you called the calibration done?"

If the bracket's off and nobody checked, the complaint lands back on your RO, and now you're explaining to an adjuster why the car went out with a blind spot system aimed at the wrong lane.
