Autopilot unavailable and nobody touched a target
From the Van · Issue #17

A Model 3 came back to a shop two days after delivery. Customer said Autopilot was grayed out and the lane stuff just quit.

Shop figured they had a bad camera. They did not.

Here is the trap with Tesla. The cameras self-calibrate as the car drives. There is no target board, no static setup, no sitting in the bay reading a wall. The car has to put real miles down before the system learns itself back in.

This one got a windshield and a front camera area disturbed during the repair. That resets the calibration. The car needs road time, often 20 to 100 miles of varied driving, before Autopilot comes back online.

The shop delivered it the same afternoon the repair finished. Car hadn't driven enough to re-learn. So the owner drove off with features dead and assumed the shop broke something.

No DTC that screams this at you. Just a gray icon and an angry phone call.

Here's what that means for you: a Tesla can leave your shop looking perfect and still have a required calibration that hasn't finished. If you deliver before the car has done its miles, the comeback lands on you, not the customer's commute.

Ask your cal shop this week: "On a Tesla, how do you confirm the self-calibration actually completed before we hand back the keys?"

The answer should involve verifying completion, not just handing it over and hoping the highway sorts it out.

The van offered to do the 100 miles itself. I said no. It sulks on road trips.
