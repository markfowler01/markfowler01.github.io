Passed the scan, failed in the parking lot
From the Van · Issue #9

A blind spot module that calibrates fine and still reads the world wrong. That one costs somebody.

Here's the pattern I see more than I'd like. A car comes in for a rear-end hit, blind spot radar behind the bumper cover gets disturbed. Shop does the R and I clean, reassembles, sublets the calibration. Radar calibrates, no codes, everybody moves on.

Then the customer calls two weeks later. The blind spot light comes on in an empty lane. Or worse, it stays dark when a car is sitting right there.

The trap is the cal bay itself. These rear radars aim by learning what's around them, and if the bay is full of metal shelving, a lift, a rolling toolbox parked eight feet off the quarter panel, the radar can learn its zero against all that steel. It passes. It's just calibrated to a lie.

Toyota's procedure calls for clearance around the vehicle during the aim for exactly this reason. A lot of vendors skip the room check because the software said done.

The fix is boring. Clear space, correct target placement, re-run it. But you only know to re-run it if somebody looked up from the scan tool.

When you sublet a rear radar calibration, ask one question: what did the space around the car look like during the aim. "It passed" is not the same as "it aimed against open air." That gap is the comeback.

The van has strong feelings about toolboxes parked in cal bays. It has seen things.
