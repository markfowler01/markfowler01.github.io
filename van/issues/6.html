No fault on the module. Fault on the bus.
From the Van · Issue #6

A 2024 Silverado came through with the blind spot warning dark and a lane change alert nobody could clear.

The shop had already priced a new blind spot module. Made sense on paper. The light was on, the system was down, swap the part.

Here's the trap. On the 2024+ GM trucks, the blind spot radars talk to the rest of the car over a serial data line. When that communication drops, the module throws a U-code, not a B-code. U means network. B means the box itself.

The part was fine. The connector at the rear bumper had a spread terminal from the repair. It read voltage but wouldn't hold data. So the module sat there healthy, saying nothing, while everybody upstream assumed it was dead.

New module goes in, same U-code, and now you've eaten the part cost and the return fight. The car still doesn't calibrate.

We pin-tested the connector before anyone ordered anything. Repaired the terminal, cleared the network fault, verified both rear radars reporting. Done.

The van is quietly smug about catching this one.

Takeaway: when you sublet a blind spot calibration on a 2024+ GM and the tech mentions a U-code, ask whether the module actually failed or just stopped talking. A network fault after a rear collision is a wiring problem until proven otherwise. Swapping the radar first is how you buy a part twice.

A module with no codes isn't always broken. Sometimes it just lost the group chat.
