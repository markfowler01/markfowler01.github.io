No codes, calibration passed, and the camera was still bad
From the Van · Issue #4

Had a 2015 CR-V come across the bench this week. Front camera, windshield replaced, needs a static calibration. Routine.

Except it wasn't.

These 2013 to 2016 Hondas have a known problem with the forward camera. The solder joints on the internal board crack over time. Heat cycles, vibration, years of Washington potholes. The joint fatigues and the camera starts dropping in and out.

Here's the trap. When it's connected, it can still calibrate. It'll pass. No stored codes. Everything looks green on the scanner.

Then the customer drives away and the lane keep quits on the freeway a week later. Comes back as a comeback. And now it looks like the calibration was bad, not the camera.

I caught it because the camera was intermittently dropping communication during the pre-scan. Flickering, not failing. If you're only looking at stored codes, you never see it.

We flagged it, the shop ordered the camera, and the car left right the first time.

The takeaway: on a 2013 to 2016 Honda with a camera complaint, a clean calibration does not mean a good camera. When you sublet the work, ask whether they watched live camera communication during the pre-scan, not just the code list. A passed calibration on a cracked-solder camera is a comeback with a delay timer on it.

The van has seen enough of these Hondas to get suspicious on sight.

— Mark
