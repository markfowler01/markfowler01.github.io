Rear hit, fresh quarter, and a module that keeps going quiet
From the Van · Issue #15

A rear-hit truck comes back from a sublet calibration marked done. A week later the blind spot light is on again. Then it's off. Then on.

This one shows up on 2024-and-up GM a lot. Silverado, Sierra, the newer Cadillacs. You fix a rear quarter or a bumper, somebody disturbs the blind spot module connector or its ground, and the module starts dropping off the vehicle network.

Here's the trap. It's intermittent. The car scans clean in the bay on a good day, the module answers, the calibration completes, everybody signs off. Then the customer hits a bump on the freeway and the connection chatters. Light comes on. No stored code half the time, because the fault clears itself before it logs.

The fix was not another calibration. It was a loose pin and a lazy ground on the module side, left behind during the rear repair. Reseat, clean the ground, verify the module stays on the bus under a wiggle test, then calibrate. One and done.

Calibrating a module that can't hold a network connection just buys you a comeback with a calibration invoice stapled to it.

Here's what that means for you: an intermittent blind spot light after rear work is a comms problem until proven otherwise. Calibrating it first hides the real fault and sends the truck back out to fail again on the customer.

Ask your cal shop this week: "Before you recalibrate that blind spot module, did you confirm it stays on the network under a wiggle test, or did it just answer once?"

The van has seen a lot of modules that answer once and then go quiet. So have your customers.
