Hybrid coolant nobody topped off after the repair
From the Van · Issue #5

A Corolla Cross hybrid came in for a front-end hit. Nothing exotic. Bumper, radar bracket, some cooling work.

The repair looked clean. Then it threw a temperature-related fault on the hybrid system before it ever got near a calibration bay.

Here's the trap. The Corolla Cross hybrid runs a separate cooling loop for the hybrid battery and power electronics. It is not the same as the engine coolant. Different fill point, different spec, different bleed procedure.

When the front end takes a hit and lines get opened or drained, that inverter loop can end up low. The engine coolant gets topped off because that's the obvious one. The hybrid loop gets forgotten.

Low coolant on that side means the system throttles or faults on temperature. And that fault will block or invalidate your ADAS work downstream, because the car won't stay in a stable state long enough to trust a calibration.

So now you've got a car that looks repaired, drives, and still bounces back. That's a comeback nobody budgeted for.

The fix was boring in the best way. Fill the correct loop to spec, bleed it per procedure, confirm temps hold, then calibrate. The van approves of boring.

Takeaway: On any hybrid or EV with front-end damage, ask whoever's doing the cooling work to confirm the hybrid battery and inverter loop, not just engine coolant. And when you sublet the calibration, ask if the car held stable temps through the procedure. A car that overheats mid-cal isn't calibrated. It's just parked.
