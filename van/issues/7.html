Calibrated fine in the bay. Failed the second it hit the road.
From the Van · Issue #7

A Lexus RX came back twice for a blind spot monitor fault. Passed calibration both times. Lit up on the test drive both times.

Here is the trap. The rear radar sensors that run BSM sit behind the bumper cover and they see metal. Not just the car's metal. Everything around them.

The first two calibrations ran in a bay packed with a rolling toolbox, a parts cart, and a floor jack parked about four feet off the rear quarter. To the radar, that is a wall. It learns the reflection as part of the baseline, passes clean, then hits the real world and starts screaming false obstacles.

We cleared the bay. Nothing metal inside the sensor's field for the distance the OEM procedure calls out. Ran it again. Passed and stayed passed.

No bad sensor. No module swap. Just a cal environment that lied to the car.

The van has opinions about calibrating anything in a room full of steel. The van is usually right.

Here is what you do with it. When you sublet a rear radar or BSM calibration, ask whoever does the work where they ran it and what was around the car. A pass in a cluttered bay is not a pass. If the sensor learned your floor jack, the comeback is on your RO, not theirs.

And a clean scan report does not mean a clean environment. Those are two different things.
