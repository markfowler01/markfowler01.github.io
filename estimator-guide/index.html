The 60-second answer

If the repair touched any of these, plan on a calibration.

  • Windshield R&R, or the camera came off the glass
  • Front bumper cover, grille, emblem, or bumper beam
  • Rear bumper cover, rear body, or quarter panel
  • Side mirror that has a camera in it
  • Liftgate or tailgate with a camera
  • Wheel alignment, struts, springs, tires a different size, lift
  • Any ADAS sensor, camera, or bracket, for any reason
  • Airbag deployed. The hit was hard enough to move structure

Subaru with EyeSight: any collision repair, anywhere on the car.Not just glass. Subaru bulletin 18-231-23. Details below.

Where the sensors live

Eight spots. Move the part, move the aim.

Every sensor is aimed at the factory to a fraction of a degree. It is aimed through, or bolted to, a part your shop repairs. That is the whole story. Calibration puts the aim back.

  1. 1
    Forward cameraOn the windshield, behind the mirror. Runs lane keep, auto brake, adaptive cruise. Moved by: glass R&R, roof or A-pillar work, an alignment.
  2. 2
    Front radarBehind the emblem or grille, on the bumper beam. Moved by: bumper cover, grille, emblem, beam, ride height.
  3. 3
    Front park sensorsIn the bumper cover. Usually verify, rarely calibrate. Wrong paint or wrong cover blinds them.
  4. 4
    Mirror cameras360 view and LaneWatch. Moved by: mirror R&R, door R&R. A blind spot light alone is not a camera.
  5. 5
    Blind spot radarsOne each corner behind the rear cover, bracketed to the quarter. Rear cross traffic uses the same two. Moved by: rear cover, quarter, rear body.
  6. 6
    Rear cameraIn the liftgate or trunk trim. Moved by: liftgate, tailgate, license trim.
  7. 7
    Rear park sensorsIn the rear cover. Verify after refinish. Ford limits rear cover refinish to 12 mils.
  8. 8
    Wheels and suspensionThrust angle decides where 1 and 2 look. Moved by: alignment, struts, springs, tires a different size, lift.

Bay or road, by make

Why this matters to your schedule.

Bay is targets on a level floor with no metal in the radar's view. We can do it at your shop, same day. Road is a drive at speed on a road with good lane lines, in daylight, usually not in rain. Plan road time before you promise delivery. This is the pattern we see most in the van. The OEM procedure for the VIN wins.

The eight red flags

When you see one of these on the RO, the question to ask is already written.

Windshield

The camera is glued to the glass. New glass, new aim. Aftermarket glass fails the calibration more often than OE, and Subaru says genuine glass only.

Ask: "Is this OE glass, and does the cal shop know it is a camera car?"

Front bumper cover

Radar looks through the cover. Extra paint, plastic weld, or a non-OE cover cuts the signal. Nothing shows in the bay. It shows on the highway.

Ask: "How many coats went on over the radar, and did the cal complete?"

Rear bumper cover

Blind spot radars live behind it, one each corner. Refinish counts. Ford: topcoat only, 12 mils max, no substrate repair over the sensors.

Ask: "Were both blind spot radars calibrated, and was the bay clear of metal?"

Grille or emblem

The front radar is usually right behind the badge. Pull the grille, you moved the radar. A non-radar emblem blocks it.

Ask: "Was the radar aimed after the grille went back on, and is that the radar emblem?"

Side mirror with a camera

Mirror cameras feed the 360 view and LaneWatch. Wrong variant mirror, no code, wrong picture. A blind spot light alone does not need a cal.

Ask: "Right part number for this trim, and was surround view calibrated?"

Alignment, suspension, tires

Thrust angle changes where the camera and radar point. Most makes require a recal after an alignment. Bigger tires or a lift change radar height.

Ask: "Alignment first, then calibration, and was the steering angle sensor reset?"

Airbag deployment

The hit was hard enough to fire the bags, so it was hard enough to move the structure the camera and radar bolt to. Treat the front systems as suspect even when the parts look fine.

Ask: "Was the front structure measured, and were camera and radar calibrated after the pull?"

Quarter, roof, liftgate

Radar brackets in the quarter, cameras in the tailgate. Subaru mounts the EyeSight camera to the roof header, so roof and pillar work moves it.

Ask: "Did the cal shop check the bracket, or just run the routine?"

Makes I see every week

Western Washington gotchas.

Subaru EyeSightAny collision repair gets the EyeSight calibration. Not just glass. A hit anywhere can tilt the body a fraction of a degree, and EyeSight sees it. Subaru bulletin 18-231-23 has us check body angle with a digital gauge before the cal. Write it on every Subaru collision RO. Spec OE glass and save yourself the second trip.
Toyota and LexusThe radar sits behind the emblem and will not throw a code when it is a little off. Clean scan, wrong radar. Front hit means radar calibration, every time, and the emblem must be the radar version.
Honda and AcuraThe forward camera aims in the bay and then on the road, in that order. LaneWatch and 360 cameras in the mirror need aiming after a mirror R&R. Honda calls for camera aiming after an alignment on many models.
Ford and LincolnFord's May 2026 statement is the clearest in the industry: calibrate on any ADAS repair, DTC or not. Rear cover refinish is topcoat only, 12 mils max over BLIS. A tire size change is a camera alignment trigger by name.
GMThe forward camera calibrates on the road. It needs miles, lane lines, and daylight, so build the drive into the delivery promise. Digital surround-view cameras need no cal; analog ones do. A dead battery can knock a front camera into relearn.
Nissan and InfinitiRadar behind the emblem. Too many coats on the cover, or an aftermarket cover, cuts the signal. You do not get a code in the bay. You get a "front radar obstruction" warning after delivery.
Hyundai, Kia, GenesisRear corner radars self-align by driving. A bracket bent two degrees still "completes" and still reads wrong on the road. Check the bracket before the drive, not after the comeback.
Jeep, Ram, ChryslerCamera on the road, radar in the bay with a reflector. Most front hits need both, and the bay work comes first. Level floor, clear bay, or the radar aim is a guess.
TeslaCameras self-calibrate over miles and there is no radar to aim. Deliver before the drive is done and the customer calls you about Autopilot the next morning.
Scan versus aim

No code does not mean calibrated.

A scan finds what is broken. It does not prove the aim is right. Those are two different questions and the scan tool only answers one of them.

Ford put it in writing on May 1, 2026: system calibration is "required for all vehicles with ADAS-related repairs, regardless of whether a Diagnostic Trouble Code (DTC) is present," and a pre-repair scan is required on every vehicle with damage, "even when no dashboard indicators are present." Most OEMs say the same thing in their own words. The links are at the bottom.

So the order is: pre-scan before the estimate is final, repair, calibrate, post-scan before delivery. If the invoice only says "scanned, no codes found," that is not a calibration and it will not protect you when the car comes back.

What the paperwork should show

Six things, or it is not done.

1. The OEM procedure by name. 2. Pre-scan and post-scan reports. 3. Target setup or aim values, before and after. 4. Bay, road, or both. 5. VIN, tech, date. 6. Any fault that was found and what was done about it.

That paperwork is what gets the line paid, and it is what keeps your shop's name off a subrogation letter six months from now.
